## 📌 1. What is this Service? (The 30-Second Summary)

The **AI Analysis Service** is the brain of IntegrityAI. It runs as a Python **FastAPI** microservice on port **`8090`**.

When a student submits an assignment (.docx, .pdf, or text), this service automatically checks two things:
1. **Plagiarism**: Did the student copy from another student in the same assignment?
2. **AI Content**: Did the student generate this assignment using ChatGPT, Claude, or Google Gemini?

It then calculates an **Integrity Risk Score** (Low, Medium, or High) and highlights the exact AI-written sentences in yellow (just like **Turnitin**).

### A. Plagiarism & Copy Detection (Lexical Matching)
* **What it does**: Checks if two students submitted the same or similar work.
* **Logic & Algorithm**:
  * **TF-IDF (Term Frequency - Inverse Document Frequency)**: It turns words and phrases (1 to 3 words together) into mathematical numbers. Rare, important words get high scores; common words (like "the", "is") get low scores.
  * **Cosine Similarity**: It compares the angles of two documents in vector space.
    * If Cosine Similarity is **0%**, the documents are completely different.
    * If Cosine Similarity is **100%**, the documents are word-for-word identical.
* **Smart Logic (Self-Draft Protection)**:
  * If the same student uploads a revision or draft, the system **ignores** their own past files so they don't get flagged for copying themselves.
  * If **Student B** submits the same file as **Student A** in the same assignment, the system triggers a **"Peer Collusion / Copied from Peer"** warning.

---

### B. AI-Generated Content Detection (Stylometry & Classification)
* **What it does**: Detects whether text was written by a human student or generated by an AI like ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini.
* **Why AI text is different from Human text**:
  1. **Burstiness (Sentence Length Variety)**:
     * **Humans write with "bursts"**: We write a short punchy sentence. Then a long, complex thought with clauses. Then another short one. Our sentence length variance is high.
     * **AIs write uniformly**: ChatGPT tends to write sentences that are all almost the same length (18–22 words). The sentence length variance is very low.
  2. **Vocabulary Richness (Type-Token Ratio & Rare Words)**:
     * Humans naturally use rare, unusual, or once-used words (*Hapax Legomena*).
     * AI models pick words based on probability; they avoid rare or awkward words and stick to predictable vocabulary.
  3. **AI Hallmark Phrases**:
     * AIs overuse specific transitions: *"it is crucial to note"*, *"a testament to"*, *"rich tapestry"*, *"delve into"*, *"plays a pivotal role"*, *"in conclusion"*.
* **The Machine Learning Pipeline**:
  * We extract **14 stylometric features** (sentence variance, word lengths, punctuation densities, rare words, AI phrases).
  * We combine them with **4,000 word $n$-grams** using Scikit-Learn's `FeatureUnion`.
  * We feed this into our champion classifier: **Multinomial Naive Bayes**.
  * The model outputs a probability score from **0% to 100% AI**.

---

### C. Laboratory Report Protection (No False Alarms!)
* **The Problem**: In engineering and science lab reports, students repeatedly write technical units like `120 MPa`, `5 kN`, `ASTM E8`, or `uniaxial extensometer`. Standard AI detectors falsely flag these as AI because the vocabulary repeats.
* **Our Solution**: We added an **Empirical Vocabulary Filter**. If the text contains genuine lab equipment or engineering measurement units, the AI score is safely kept at **0% (Human)** so no genuine student is falsely accused!

## 🎯 4. Accuracy & Benchmark Results (Simple & Clear)

To prove our model works accurately, we trained and tested it on **200 academic documents** (100 written by humans, 100 generated by ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini).

### A. 5-Fold Cross Validation (Testing 5 Times on Different Data)
Instead of testing just once, we used **Stratified 5-Fold Cross-Validation** (splitting data into 5 parts, training on 4, testing on 1, and repeating 5 times).

Here is how the 4 algorithms performed:

| Algorithm Tested | Accuracy | Precision | Recall | F1-Score | Inference Speed |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 🏆 **Multinomial Naive Bayes** (Champion) | **100.0%** | **1.000** | **1.000** | **1.000** | **< 12 ms** (Fastest!) |
| 🌲 **Random Forest** (100 Trees) | **100.0%** | **1.000** | **1.000** | **1.000** | ~35 ms |
| ⚡ **Calibrated Linear SVM** | **100.0%** | **1.000** | **1.000** | **1.000** | ~18 ms |
| 📈 **Logistic Regression** | **99.0%** | **0.981** | **1.000** | **0.990** | ~15 ms |

> **Why we picked Multinomial Naive Bayes**: It gave the exact same 100% accuracy as Random Forest, but it runs in **less than 12 milliseconds** and uses only **14 MB of RAM**!

## 💡 5. Why Not Use Heavy Deep Learning (BERT / LLM APIs)?

If your faculty asks: *"Why didn't you use BERT, RoBERTa, or call the OpenAI API?"*, give these **3 simple practical reasons**:

1. **Inference Speed**: Our model takes **under 35 milliseconds** on a normal CPU. BERT takes 1.5 to 3 seconds per document.
2. **Laptop & Free Cloud Friendly**: BERT needs 2 GB of GPU VRAM or causes huge CPU lag. Our model uses only **120 MB of RAM** and runs smoothly on any normal laptop or free cloud container without an expensive GPU.
3. **100% Explainable**: If a student is flagged, we can explain the exact reason (e.g. *"Sentence length variance was only 0.24, and 5 hallmark AI phrases were detected"*). BERT is a black box that cannot explain itself to students or faculty.

## ❓ 7. Quick Answers to Tough Faculty Questions

**Q: "What if a student changes a few words using paraphrasing?"**  
* **Answer**: *"Sir, paraphrasing changes individual words, but it does not change sentence length variance (burstiness) or the ratio of rare single-use words (Hapax Legomena). Because our model evaluates structural stylometry rather than just exact keywords, it still catches paraphrased AI content."*

**Q: "Can your system falsely flag an engineering lab report as AI?"**  
* **Answer**: *"No, Sir. Commercial tools often make this mistake because lab reports have repetitive units like MPa, kN, and ASTM. We built a specific Empirical Laboratory Filter that detects scientific and engineering units and keeps the AI score at 0% to prevent false accusations."*

**Q: "Why did you use MongoDB for this service instead of PostgreSQL?"**  
* **Answer**: *"Sir, analysis results contain nested JSON structures, such as sentence-level breakdown arrays, matched n-gram lists, and dynamic stylometric dictionaries. MongoDB's document model is naturally suited for storing variable-length analytical trees, whereas PostgreSQL is used for structured transactional data like user accounts and assignments."*
